Duendes in Baños, Ecuador

A contextual guide to one of the most visible contemporary cultural phenomena in Baños, without confusing documented artistic work with ancient folklore.

Contemporary cultural phenomenon

Why duendes became visible in Baños

Duende figures have become one of the most recognisable images in contemporary Baños. They appear in handmade sculpture, artistic environments, tourism businesses and stories circulated among visitors.

This visibility is recent and layered. It is more accurate to distinguish documented artistic projects, broader European and Latin American folklore references, local tourism narratives and later commercial imitation.

Translation note: this guide keeps the Spanish word duende because “goblin,” “elf” and “gnome” do not fully reproduce its cultural range.

Context

Four different layers

Folklore

Older duende-like beings exist in many traditions, but their meanings vary by place and period.

Artistic creation

Contemporary artists can develop a documented visual language, body of work and visitor experience.

Tourism narrative

Destinations often simplify or combine stories to create memorable attractions.

Commercial reproduction

Successful imagery may be copied, industrialised or detached from its original authorship and context.

Evaluate an experience

Questions that reveal cultural coherence

  • Is there a named creator or documented project history?
  • Are the objects handmade or mass-produced?
  • Does the place explain its sources and influences?
  • Does it distinguish folklore from contemporary creation?
  • Are images and narratives copied without attribution?

What authenticity means here

Authenticity does not require claiming an ancient origin. A contemporary project can be culturally valuable when its authorship, process, intention and continuity are transparent.

Tourism and ethics

Credit, context and institutional responsibility

When public institutions promote themed attractions, they influence which narratives appear legitimate. Transparent selection, attribution and public calls reduce the risk of favouritism or the erasure of documented creators.

Visitors also contribute by checking sources, recognising handmade work and avoiding claims that turn recent marketing into supposed ancestral tradition.
