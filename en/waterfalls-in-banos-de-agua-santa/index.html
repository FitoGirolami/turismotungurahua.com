Waterfalls in Baños de Agua Santa

The Waterfall Route is one of the most recognisable journeys in Tungurahua. This guide explains the main stops and the conditions visitors should consider.

Water in motion

The landscape that defines Baños

The concentration of waterfalls along the Pastaza valley is one of the main reasons Baños became a major nature destination. The route east toward Río Verde reveals the steep relief and the transition toward the Amazon basin.

Visitors can explore by bicycle, vehicle, organised transport or a combination of short walks and viewpoint stops.

Conditions change

Rain, water levels, road conditions and crowding affect the experience. Check local information on the day of travel.

Main route

The Waterfall Route

The best-known corridor connects Baños with Río Verde and a sequence of waterfalls and viewpoints.

Pailón del Diablo

The most emblematic waterfall, known for its force, humidity and close viewpoints. Paths can be wet and physically demanding.

Manto de la Novia

A recognised waterfall and scenic stop, often associated with cable-car crossings over the valley.

Agoyán

A major element of the river landscape and an important reference along the eastern route.

Practical advice

What to carry

  • Light waterproof clothing.
  • Shoes with good grip.
  • Protection for phones and cameras.
  • Water and enough time to avoid rushing.

Choose the route for your group

Not every stop has the same level of stairs, humidity or physical effort. Families and travellers with limited mobility should check access conditions before arriving.

Complete the day

Beyond the waterfalls

A waterfall route can be physically intense. Many visitors balance it with hot springs, a calm walk through town or a cultural experience rather than adding another demanding activity.
