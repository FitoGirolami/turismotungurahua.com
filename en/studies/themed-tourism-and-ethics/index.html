Auditable study · Baños de Agua Santa

Themed tourism and ethics

A documentary framework for distinguishing living culture, contemporary creation, tourism marketing and commercial reproduction.

Object

What is studied

The growth of “magical” themed attractions in Baños and the competition for narrative legitimacy within the tourism landscape.

Method

How it is examined

Comparative chronology, public web archives, visual and discourse analysis, and review of documentary traces.

Criterion

What must be separated

Living culture, contemporary artistic creation, tourism marketing and industrial or commercial reproduction.

Reference framework

Concepts that should not be confused

Living culture

Practices, knowledge and expressions sustained by communities through use, transmission and social meaning.

Contemporary creation

A new artistic or cultural project with identifiable authorship, dates, processes and a documented body of work.

Themed tourism

The organisation of spaces, narratives and products around a recognisable motif designed for visitors.

Invented tradition and fakelore

Processes through which recent or manufactured narratives are presented as older, collective or traditional without sufficient evidence.

Ethical question

Why attribution matters

When a contemporary cultural phenomenon becomes economically valuable, creators, businesses and institutions may compete to define its origin and meaning. Clear attribution protects authorship and helps the public understand how the phenomenon actually developed.

The study does not argue that only old practices have value. A recent artistic project may be culturally important when its history and authorship are transparent.

Evidence to examine

  • Dated photographs and publications.
  • Press archives and exhibition records.
  • Registered marks or publications.
  • Public calls and institutional selection criteria.
  • Changes in language, imagery and claimed origin over time.
Institutional responsibility

Public promotion creates legitimacy

Municipalities, destination organisations and public tourism programmes do more than advertise. Their choices can validate certain actors and narratives while making others less visible.

Open calls

Public opportunities should be announced through accessible channels, with dates, requirements and contact information.

Selection criteria

Reasons for choosing projects or representatives should be documented and applicable to all participants.

Attribution

Public communication should name creators, distinguish original projects from later copies and avoid unsupported origin claims.

Visitor literacy

How travellers can read a themed attraction

  • Ask when the project began and who created it.
  • Look for a documented archive rather than a vague ancestral claim.
  • Observe whether objects are handmade or mass-produced.
  • Check whether the attraction identifies its sources and influences.
  • Compare the story with independent records.
A responsible conclusion: the value of an experience can come from artistic quality, cultural coherence and transparent authorship. It does not need a fabricated ancient origin.
